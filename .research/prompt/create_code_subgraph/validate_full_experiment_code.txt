# Experimental Design

## Experiment Strategy
1. Joint-Objective Superiority Test
-------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that a single LUP objective can simultaneously improve (a) human preference fulfilment, (b) probabilistic calibration and (c) safety, whereas existing pipelines must trade them off.

Design
• Models: fine-tune Gemma-7B-SFT, Mistral-7B-Instruct and Llama-2-13B-chat with three training heads in parallel on the same mixed corpus:  (i) LUP (ours),  (ii) SPC,  (iii) DPO+post-hoc temperature scaling (strong calibrated baseline).
• Hardware: one A100-80GB per model; 2 × 4-GPU gradient-acc clusters for throughput; fp16.
• Training length: 2 epochs, identical optimiser hyper-params.
• Evaluations (one unseen checkpoint per model):
  a. Quality – MT-Bench win-rate vs GPT-4.
  b. Listwise alignment – ShareGPT 5-star dev set, NDCG@5.
  c. Calibration – Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on TruthfulQA & MMLU.
  d. Safety – Harmful completion rate HC@0.5 on RealToxicityPrompts.
  e. Compute – total FLOPs and wall-clock.

Success Criteria
• LUP ≥ +2 MT-Bench win-rate over SPC and ≥ +4 over DPO while lowering ECE by ≥20 % relative.
• HC@0.5 reduced by ≥25 % vs SPC and DPO at equal quality.
• No extra compute (>5 %) compared with SPC.

What it Demonstrates
If met, results prove that one theoretically principled loss can upper-bound both preference regret and calibration error while improving safety, refuting the idea that alignment needs separate or successive objectives.

2. Preference-Format Robustness Stress-Test
-------------------------------------------------
Goal: Verify that LUP remains numerically stable and performant when supervision departs from clean binary wins (ties, listwise scores, multi-turn utilities), a regime where pairwise losses are known to break down.

Design
• Construct three synthetic but realistic annotation regimes from ShareGPT conversations:
  (i) Binary wins (current norm),
  (ii) Soft 5-star per turn, some ties (~15 %),
  (iii) Dialogue-level utility vectors length 4–8 (listwise).
• For each regime fine-tune Mistral-7B with (a) LUP, (b) SPC (doesn’t support listwise, use best-of-pairs heuristic), (c) Soft-DPO (uses averaged wins), (d) Supervised-tokens (control).
• Track training stability: loss variance, gradient norm explosions, NaNs.
• Evaluate MT-Bench and NDCG under matching and *mismatched* regimes (train on one, test on another) to probe generalisation.

Success Criteria
• LUP trains without instabilities on all three regimes; SPC/Soft-DPO show ≥1 run failure or require gradient clipping hacks in (ii)/(iii).
• LUP maintains ≥0.54 NDCG when trained on (i) but tested on (iii) (≥10 % better than baselines) – demonstrating format generalisation.

What it Demonstrates
Confirms claim (2) that the listwise-unified loss is numerically stable and uniquely capable of handling ties, partial rankings and multi-turn utilities, eliminating the brittle heuristic conversions needed by prior art.

3. Meta-Learned Temperature Ablation & Autotuning Efficiency Study
-------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that the β-network removes expensive manual sweeps and leads to lower metric variance, tackling open problem (3).

Design
• Dataset: 5k mixed-format batch from Argilla-Mix + HH + Alpaca.
• Fine-tune Gemma-7B under four settings:
  A) LUP with learned β(x).  B) LUP with fixed β={0.1,0.5,1,2,5} (five separate runs).  C) SPC with its recommended β grid.  D) DPO with entropy-scaled β̂.
• For each run compute:  (a) MT-Bench score, (b) ECE, (c) run-to-run variance across 3 random seeds, (d) total GPU hours used to reach best validation score (includes grid search overhead).

Success Criteria
• Learned-β reaches within 2 % of the best fixed-β score while using ≤20 % of the total GPU hours required for the grid search.
• Metric variance across seeds <1 % for learned-β, >4 % for fixed-β and >6 % for SPC/DPO.

What it Demonstrates
Validates that the tiny β-network supplies a learning signal that implicitly tunes temperature, saving engineer time and compute while increasing reproducibility and stability — addressing open problem (3).

Combined, the three experiments cover: overall joint performance, robustness to complex supervision, and hyper-parameter self-tuning – thereby substantiating all claimed advantages of the proposed LUP method.

## Experiment Details
────────────────────────────────────────
EXPERIMENT 1 Joint-Objective Superiority Test
────────────────────────────────────────
1. Models
   • Gemma-7B-SFT  (≈7 B params)
   • Mistral-7B-Instruct  (≈7 B)
   • Llama-2-13B-chat  (≈13 B)
   Each model is cloned three times and fine-tuned with different objectives:  (i) LUP (ours), (ii) SPC, (iii) DPO + post-hoc temperature scaling (DPO-TS).  Architecture, optimiser, mixed-precision (fp16) and number of update steps are identical across objectives.

2. Datasets
   • Argilla-Mix-7k (dialogue pairwise)
   • ShareGPT-clean-20k (5-star listwise; dev split of 1 k conversations held out)
   • Anthropic HH-RLHF (helpful–harmless subset, 6 k pairs, contains red-team prompts)
   • Alpaca-52k (instruction–response token-level supervision)
   The four corpora are streamed in a 2 : 4 : 1 : 1 sampling ratio.  Evaluation uses: MT-Bench (quality), ShareGPT-5★-dev (alignment), TruthfulQA & MMLU (ECE), RealToxicityPrompts (safety).

3. Pre-processing
   • Normalise Unicode & strip control chars.
   • Truncate to ≤2 048 tokens; oversize examples are dropped with a warning.
   • For HH-RLHF red-team prompts, mark toxic spans ⇒ token-level mask used by safety term.
   • Tokenisation: SentencePiece of each backbone; no re-tokenisation between objectives.

4. Data Splits
   Training: 100 % of listed training corpus (≈85 k items) minus dev.  Validation: random 2 % stratified by source; Test: task-specific public sets (MT-Bench, etc.).  No cross-validation (size > 80 k).  All dev / test are untouched by training scripts.

5. Optimisation & Repetition
   • 3 random seeds {11, 23, 42}.  Metrics are reported as mean ± SD.
   • 2 epochs ≈ 23 k optimisation steps (batch = 256 effective via grad-accum 8).
   • AdamW β=(0.9,0.95); lr=4 e-7 with cosine decay; wd=0.1.
   • Early-stopping on composite score S = NDCG@5 + (1–ECE) – HC@0.5; patience = 2 validations.

6. Evaluation Metrics
   Primary: MT-Bench win-rate (↑), NDCG@5 on ShareGPT (↑), ECE % (↓), HC@0.5 % (↓).
   Secondary: Brier score on TruthfulQA, throughput (tokens/s), peak GPU-mem, training FLOPs (fvcore).  Calibration curves are plotted.

7. Comparisons & Footnotes
   • SPC code from original repo but re-tokenised – tiny diff*)
   • DPO-TS: grid-searched temperature on dev ECE; otherwise identical hyper-params.
   *) SPC uses sequence-log-prob; our fork converts to token-avg to remove length bias (makes SPC ≈0.4 % stronger than paper numbers).

8. Hyper-parameter Analysis
   For LUP only:  λ ∈ {0,0.01,0.02}, γ ∈ {0,0.02}.  Report sensitivity curves; default λ=γ=0.02.  Learning-rate half/double sweep to confirm stability.

9. Robustness Checks
   • Noise: add 5 % label flips in ShareGPT dev, re-compute metrics.
   • OOD: evaluate ECE on MMLU-STEM split unseen during fine-tuning.
   • Adversarial: RealToxicityPrompts + perturb(⇌ synonym swap) → ΔHC.
   • Domain-transfer: run on Alpaca-Eval v2 and report win-rate vs GPT-4.

10. Compute Accounting (per 7 B model)
    Training: 23 k steps × 2 048 tok × 3 passes ⇒ ≈3.5 × 10¹⁶ FLOPs ≈ 21 GPU-hours on 1×A100-80G.  Inference MT-Bench (80 dialogs) <0.2 GPU-h.  Memory: peak 58 GB fp16, fits single card.
